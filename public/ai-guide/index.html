Team guide

Use your own AI to make proposals and pages

You can now ask the AI app you already use, like Muse, Claude or ChatGPT, to build proposals, landing pages, sponsor pages and QR signs for you. It works like asking Pro in Slack. Your AI makes the real thing on proposals.getshortcut.co, saves it under your name, and gives you the link.

Setting it up takes about five minutes, once. This page explains what it can do, what it won't do, and how to connect it.

What you can ask it to do

Type or say it the way you would to a coworker. These are real requests and what comes back.

After a call

Make a proposal for Acme: chair massage at their New York office, 350 5th Ave, on November 12, 4 hours with 2 therapists.

A live proposal link in about a minute, with 24 appointments at $1,200. It is saved as a draft under your name.

Staffing math

Acme wants about 40 massages. How many therapists and hours do I need?

A few staffing options, each with its exact price, so you can pick one before you build the proposal.

Changes

Move the Acme proposal to November 19 and make it quarterly.

The same link, updated with the new date and the 10% discount for four visits a year.

Two versions

Add a second version for Acme that includes headshots.

A second option on the same link, so the client can compare the two and choose.

Holiday packages

Make Acme a Merry and Bright holiday proposal for 50 people on December 10 at 350 5th Ave.

A holiday proposal at the holiday page's price: $7,605 after the 10% for combining services, with the free 30 minute class shown to the client.

Holiday pricing

What would Sleigh the Stress cost for 80 people?

The price, worked out the way the holiday page works it out, with nothing created. Ask it to make the proposal when you're ready.

Conference organizers

Make a sponsor page for the AACSB Deans Conference, October 19 to 21, 2026, massage and headshots only.

A live sponsor page for the organizer, sent from you, with your name on its booking button.

Leave-behinds

Make a landing page for Acme's wellness team.

A page about Shortcut with Acme's logo on it, ready to send after a call.

It can also make printable QR signs for event days, find a client's logo, and look up past proposals for a client.

Why this helps

You can do it from your phone

Muse and Claude both have phone apps. You can send a proposal from the train right after a call, without opening the calculator.

The numbers are right

Your AI uses the same pricing calculator as the app and Pro, so prices, staffing and discounts match what you would get by hand.

It can use what your AI already knows

If your AI can read your email or notes, you can say "make a proposal from what Dana asked for in her last email" and it will pull the details from there.

Everything lands in the usual places

What your AI makes is saved in the app with everything else, and new proposals post in #proposals like any other.

What it won't do

Your AI can't send email or messages, look up or change leads, create invoices, or delete anything. Those stay with you, and with Pro in Slack, where a person always does the sending.

Proposals are saved as drafts. Open the link and check it before you send it to a client, the same as you would with one you made yourself.

Set it up

  1. 1

    Get your key from Will

    Your key is a private password made just for your AI. It lets your AI create things on the site as you. Will sends it to you privately. If you connect more than one AI app, ask for a separate key for each one.

  2. 2

    Connect your AI app

    Pick the app you use.

    1. Open Muse and start a new chat.

    2. Copy this message and send it as it is. Don't add your key to it.

      Build a custom connector to Shortcut's proposals MCP server at https://proposals.getshortcut.co/.netlify/functions/mcp. It's a hosted MCP server over streamable HTTP and needs an Authorization: Bearer header. Test every tool, then save it as a skill.

      The message tells Muse where to connect and how to sign in. You don't need to understand it.

    3. Muse opens a separate, secure box that asks for a credential. Paste your key there, and only there. If the box asks for the full header value, type the word Bearer, then a space, then your key.
    4. Muse tests the connection and saves it. Then open Muse's settings and set this connection to ask you before every action, so nothing gets created or changed without your OK.
    1. On claude.ai or in the Claude desktop app, open Settings, then Connectors, and choose Add custom connector.

    2. Name it Shortcut. In the URL box, paste this link, then replace YOUR_KEY with your key.

      https://proposals.getshortcut.co/.netlify/functions/mcp?key=YOUR_KEY
    3. Leave the other fields empty and save it.
    4. In a new chat, open the tools menu under the message box and switch Shortcut on.

    Your key is part of this link, so treat the whole link like a password.

    1. In ChatGPT on the web, open Settings, then Apps & Connectors, then the advanced settings, and turn on developer mode. Not every plan has it. If you don't see it, tell Will.

    2. Create a new connector named Shortcut. Paste this link with your key in place of YOUR_KEY, and set authentication to none.

      https://proposals.getshortcut.co/.netlify/functions/mcp?key=YOUR_KEY
    3. In a new chat, turn on developer mode from the tools menu and switch Shortcut on.

    ChatGPT renames its menus often. If yours look different, look for "connectors" or "developer mode" in settings. Your key is part of the link, so treat the link like a password.

    Cursor, Claude Code, Zapier, n8n and your own scripts all work too. Tell Will what you use and he'll help you connect it. The technical details are at the bottom of this page.

  3. 3

    Check that it works

    Ask your AI: "List the latest sponsor pages." If it answers with a list of conference pages, you're connected.

Getting good results

  • Put the basics in one message: the client, the service, the date, the address, how many hours and how many people. If something is missing, it will ask.
  • Start a new chat with "read the Shortcut guide first." Your AI then knows the services, prices and rules before it builds anything.
  • It can find a client's logo for a proposal, and it shows you the logo before adding it.
  • Sponsor pages go out from Will, Jaimie, Marc or Caren. That person's name goes on the page's booking button, and calls book on their calendar.
  • Use the link your AI gives you. Don't retype it.

Keep your key safe

  • Treat your key like a password. Anyone who has it can create and change live client pages as you.
  • Only paste it where the steps above say to. Never put it in a normal chat message, an email or a shared doc.
  • If you think someone else saw it, tell Will. He can switch it off in seconds and send you a new one.

Technical details, for Cursor, Claude Code and scripts

Connector (MCP, streamable HTTP): https://proposals.getshortcut.co/.netlify/functions/mcp

Send the key as a header, Authorization: Bearer YOUR_KEY, or add ?key=YOUR_KEY to the URL for apps that only take a link.

The same key works with the HTTP API at https://proposals.getshortcut.co/.netlify/functions/generate-proposal. Payloads and examples are in docs/AI_INTEGRATION.md in the calculator6 repo.
