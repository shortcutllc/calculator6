## The one idea

Most group health plans on Cigna, Aetna or Anthem include an employer wellness fund. It renews
every plan year and unused balances do not roll over. Most of it goes unspent, because the employer
does not know it exists or has no reliable vendor to spend it with. Shortcut is that vendor, and
does the paperwork. The broker gets a concrete win to bring to renewal.

## The funds

| Carrier | Fund name | How money moves |
|---|---|---|
| Cigna | Health Improvement Fund | Client pays, Cigna reimburses the client after the visit |
| Aetna | Wellness Allowance | Often pays Shortcut directly, so the client never fronts the cost |
| Anthem | Wellness Fund | Reimburses the client after the visit |

Who tends to have one (carrier funds reference): Cigna level funded groups of 51 to 2,999
employees; Aetna 101 plus, especially self funded; Anthem 51 plus (100 plus in OH, KY, IN). The
person who approves spend at Cigna is the Health Engagement Consultant. No other carrier may be
claimed. Never state a percent or dollar amount, never say "preapproved" without the client's
written preapproval, never imply a partnership with a carrier.

## What the fund pays for

Carriers reimburse two categories. Stress management: chair or table massage, assisted stretch,
mindfulness, sound bath. Group exercise: yoga, strength and sculpt, dance cardio, somatic movement,
and one on one or group nutrition coaching. Not covered: hair, makeup, nails, facials, headshots.
Those carry a 10 percent broker partner rate instead.

## What the broker and the client have to do

The broker introduces us. Shortcut then: sends the carrier consultant the preapproval details in
the language they approve; runs the visit on site, open to the whole team; formats the invoice,
W-9 and participation summary the way the carrier needs, copying the broker; the fund pays. The
client approves a date and does nothing else.

## Why a broker cares

- A renewal story: value found inside a plan the client already pays for, with participation
  numbers. 90 percent of the appointments we open fill; 87 percent of clients rebook.
- One vendor across the whole book: one vetted network in all 50 states. Local teams in New York,
  Boston, Philadelphia, Miami, Los Angeles, San Francisco and Las Vegas.
- Remote employees are covered: mindfulness, sound baths, yoga, strength, dance cardio, somatic
  movement and nutrition coaching run live on a call.
- Law firm clients: a one hour accredited Ethics CLE on mindfulness in NY, PA and FL.
- The alternative most funds get spent on is an app login nobody uses, with nothing to show at
  renewal.

## Price shape (for call prep, never in cold outreach)

Appointment services 150 dollars per Pro per hour, one time; 10 percent off quarterly, 15 percent
off monthly. Group sessions are fixed price per session (30 minute mindfulness or sound bath 1,250;
dance cardio or strength 1,000; chair yoga 650). Headshots are priced separately. Holiday offer:
10 percent off when a visit combines two or more services, a free 30 minute class to claim by March
2027, book by Dec 7.

## What makes a firm a good fit (what to look for on its site)

1. Clients with 100 plus lives, or the words level funded, self funded, mid market, large group.
2. It names Cigna, Aetna or Anthem, or says it is carrier independent with group medical.
3. It talks about wellness, wellbeing, engagement or renewal strategy, or has a wellness person.
4. Law firm, tech, finance or healthcare clients (where Shortcut already has proof).
5. Offices or clients in one of the seven local team cities.
6. It is still independent: not acquired, not a national.
Weak fit: mostly individual, family and Medicare business; fewer than 50 lives; PEO only.
